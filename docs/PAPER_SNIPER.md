# Telegram-approved paper launch entries

This implements automatic **paper** entry under an explicit, immutable Telegram-approved plan. It does not sign, submit or purchase real tokens. `ENABLE_TRADING` and `ENABLE_TX_SUBMISSION` remain false. `PAPER_SNIPER_ENABLED` controls plan APIs/evaluation and the targeted collector; it does not arm a plan.

## Use with the Grok scout

Keep the daily testnet scout in Grok. Transfer a selected project by using `/launch @account` or `/launch project.com` in Telegram, review the DD and establish its intended mainnet deployer. The Grok automation is not connected directly to this API and cannot arm plans.

Robinhood official documentation confirms mainnet **4663** and testnet **46630**: [network configuration](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain/connecting/), checked 2026-09-11. A testnet contract address never establishes its mainnet equivalent. An owner, creator, first funder and shared CREATE2 factory are different roles; keep their evidence separate. A balance lookup failure must be unknown, not zero. Missing search results mean mainnet launch status is unknown, not proven unlaunched. A scout score is not execution authority.

1. `/snipe @account ETH_BUDGET MAX_PRICE_ETH DEPLOYER [HOURS] [GAS_ETH]` creates a reviewable policy-v2 paper plan. Use the intended mainnet deployer address; a shared CREATE2 or Pons factory is rejected. All inputs remain untrusted until independent identity checks pass. `GAS_ETH` defaults to 0.0001 ETH and can only be supplied after `HOURS`.
2. Read the card and press **Arm paper plan** within ten minutes. This pins the project domain, expected deployer, mainnet network, exact curve spend, maximum gross unit price, 10% maximum immediate round-trip loss before gas, full paper gas allowance, and expiry (1–720h; default 24h). Existing immutable version-1 plans keep their original fixed-fee and $1,000 indexed-liquidity terms; they never silently become route-settled plans.
3. Arming requires collection and chain monitoring already enabled. It atomically reserves the curve spend **plus the full gas allowance**; manual buys and other plans cannot spend that reservation. At most five plans can be armed, one per project. No production plan is automatically created or armed during deployment.
4. During each plan's first ten minutes, the collector checks on ten-second ticks; after that it continues about every five minutes until the approved expiry. Up to five due deployers share a head read and one Pons V2 event query using exact indexed deployer topics. A synchronized slow batch typically adds about 576 RPC requests/day plus chain-ID checks, retries and catch-up; staggered plans can cost more. This shares the 2,000-request daily cap with other chain work, so it is not a guaranteed full-day allowance. Provider cooldown, global pause and cancellation still apply. No eligible plan means no extra RPC calls. Cursors and in-flight leases persist across restarts.
5. The existing DD/identity pipeline must find the exact official mainnet CA declaration, match canonical direct/Pons V2 deployment and obtain source review through Telegram. A deployer-only match is never enough. The evaluator requires exactly one current reviewed claim, the approved deployer/domain, fresh identity evidence and a canonical block timestamp after arming. It never repurposes testnet verification as mainnet verification.
6. The API evaluates armed plans every five seconds using database snapshots. Once identity passes, policy v2 automatically queues the bounded Pons V2 buy/sell simulation at most once per plan per five minutes. DEX indexing is not required for entry. The route report must be fresh, passing, native-ETH, bytecode-pinned, no older than the reviewed deployment, and cryptographically bound to the immutable plan plus the current reviewed source/deployment. There is no fixed-fee fallback. Entry expires ten minutes after deployment. A token already held or filled by a prior snipe is not bought again.
7. The simulation's gross spend divided by its exact token output must remain below the approved unit-price limit. Immediate simulated resale must recover at least 90% of spend before gas. Its estimated **buy execution gas** must remain within `GAS_ETH`; L1 data fees are not measured. A successful plan records the route-derived token quantity, base fee and creator/launch tax. Paper accounting conservatively charges the entire approved gas allowance rather than presenting an RPC estimate as an actual fee. The proposal, fill, position, cash debit and plan transition commit atomically. Concurrency/restarts cannot double-fill; failures roll back all financial records.
8. `/snipes` and persistent Telegram alerts show route status and the action required. `/balance` includes spend plus gas in reservations. `/positions` and `/history` identify route-priced entries and their charged allowance. P&L remains unavailable until a supported market quote appears.
9. A manual sell from a route-priced Pons position queues an exact reserve quote. Telegram shows the current reserve-impact proceeds, base fee and creator tax. Confirmation queues a second fresh quote; only that result may settle the paper fill, and it must remain above the preview's minimum. Collection and chain scanning must be on for these paid RPC checks.

`/stop`, `/chainoff` and `/run` cancel armed plans. `/chainon` does not re-arm them. Cancellation and automatic fill use the same book lock, so a stop that commits first prevents a subsequent fill. A fill already committed cannot be undone by stop. Paper reservation expiry does not alter recorded cash.

## Limits

This is a conditional paper entry and bounded launch monitor, not first-block execution. Real latency includes provider availability, polling cadence, website/X refresh, source review, twelve-confirmation identity checks and market indexing. A ten-second event poll is not a ten-second fill promise. Public RPC endpoints are rate-limited and not recommended for production by Robinhood; the existing configured provider remains unchanged.

Targeted reads cover at most 20,000 blocks with a 32-block overlap. A new plan starts with a bounded 20,000-block lookback; it is not an archival scanner. Existing cursors never jump forward over an unread range. A range-limit error allows one smaller 2,000-block retry; successful partial scans continue on ten-second ticks within the shared budget. Provider errors or malformed responses do not advance cursors. Smaller provider limits, long outages and exhausted budgets can still prevent timely detection; the ten-minute entry window is never extended to manufacture a late fill.

## Unknown launch date and command example

`/launch @account` creates an ongoing research watch with no scheduled expiry while enabled; collection must also be enabled. If the deployer is still unknown, keep this watch and review its DD. It does not authorize purchases. Once the intended mainnet deployer is established, use `/snipe @account` for the full input guide.

Example layout, with illustrative amounts: `/snipe @account 0.01 0.000001 DEPLOYER_WALLET 168 0.0001`. Replace `DEPLOYER_WALLET` with the actual intended mainnet deployer address. This reserves 0.0101 paper ETH: up to 0.01 ETH sent to the curve plus a 0.0001 ETH gas allowance, valid for seven days. The maximum price is per token and excludes gas. The hours value must come before the optional gas value. Expiry releases the reservation; the research watch continues, and renewed trade authorization requires another owner-approved plan.

`/snipe` explains each field, the difference between total spend and unit price, and the sequence from discovery to draft, chain monitoring, arming and identity review. Cards explain blocking checks and their next actions. Nothing in the guide automatically resumes collection, trusts a candidate or arms a plan.

Pons V2 native-ETH curves are the only route-settled entry and exit adapter. ERC-20 quote assets, graduated Pons V4 pools and other launchpads are blocked. Existing direct-deployment identity evidence can qualify a plan, but it still needs the supported Pons V2 factory route; direct-deployer transaction discovery is absent. The entry adapter simulates immediate buy and resale. The exit adapter uses verified constant-product math against exact canonical-block reserves and rejects changed bytecode, issuer, factory, quote asset, token balance or graduation state. It does not predict ordering changes after the quote. Exit gas, L1 data fees, mempool/sequencer strategies, isolated live signing and automatic exit policies remain missing. No live success/profitability claim is made.

Collection remains stopped and chain monitoring off during development/deployment. Enabling the feature makes commands available; the owner must separately resume collection and approve any plan.

## Validation

Migrations `0015_paper_snipes.sql`, `0016_route_checks.sql` and `0017_route_paper_exits.sql` provide immutable terms and durable entry/exit route jobs. Unit tests cover reservations, exact buy/sell route arithmetic, bindings, report freshness, gas/price limits and Telegram disclosure. Isolated database tests cover automatic queue deduplication, no fallback fills, identity changes, stale/failed reports, atomic rollback, concurrent and restarted exactly-once settlement, exact quantities/costs, proportional exit cost allocation, stop cancellation and the unchanged version-1 regression. Fake-RPC tests cover pinned bytecode/ABI/event checks, exact reserve exits and route-job leases.
